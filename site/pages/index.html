M11 take-home activity

Judge the Model

Train four classifiers plus a baseline on two health datasets, evaluate them on a test set, and build one app, in Streamlit or Node.js, that helps a clinical lead {{help clinical-lead}} decide whether any model is fit for each job. An AI agent writes the code; you make the decisions.

Which classifier would you trust for this job, at what threshold, and with what limits?

How you work with your agent

  • Your agent writes the code: the environment setup, the model-training module, a script that launches your app, and the app's three sections. It reads AGENT_BRIEF.md, which holds its spec and rules.
  • You make the clinical decisions: what matters for each job, which model and threshold you would recommend, and what your app shows the clinical lead.
  • You verify: you run the checks and the app yourself, and test every section with its reviewer task.
  • You write your own responses: DECISIONS.md and the Canvas answers are yours, not the agent's.

Start your agent with the starting prompt. Each later page has a short task to give it.

Start here

  1. Set up
    • Download the starter, create a public GitHub repository {{help github-backup}}, and give your agent the starting prompt. It sets up the environment.
    • Have it write run_app.sh for Streamlit or Node.js, then run bash run_app.sh yourself. Publish the untouched starter. It shows baseline-only results.
    Done when: python check.py --stage setup passes, the app runs on your computer, and your app link loads in a private window.
  2. Decide what matters
    • Read both dataset scenarios: 30-day readmission and diabetes screening.
    • Then, before you look at any results, fill in Before results in DECISIONS.md {{help decisions-md}} yourself: the positive class for each job, and which error matters more. Commit and push.
    Done when: the commit is on GitHub.
  3. Train the models and run the analysis
    • Have your agent write analysis/models.py to the spec. Models
    • Have it run python -m analysis, fix any error and explain the results. Analysis
    Done when: you run python check.py --stage results and it passes. Commit and push results/.
  4. Build your app
    • Give your agent each section's task, so it builds the three sections to their required minimums.
    • Run the app yourself and do each section's reviewer task. Commit and push, then test in a private window.
    Done when: every section works for both datasets and the four classifiers plus a baseline.
  5. Write your recommendations
    • Fill in Recommendations in DECISIONS.md yourself: for each job, a model and threshold, or “none is adequate”, with a reason.
    • Fill in Design choice and AI use: one thing you chose to show or leave out for your clinical lead, and one AI suggestion you turned down.
    • Rerun python -m analysis (or have your agent do it) so your app shows them, then commit and push.
    Done when: python check.py passes.
  6. Answer the Canvas questions
    • Four answers of 100–150 words each, in your own words, citing numbers from your app. The questions are on Canvas.
    • Submit your repository link.

How your work is judged

Most of the credit is for reasoning: 40 points for your four Canvas answers, each using your own numbers, tied to the job and stating the uncertainty. Your app earns 25 for meeting the three required minimums in a way a clinical lead can read cold, and 5 more for one design choice of your own, explained in DECISIONS.md. A valid analysis earns 15, and your decisions 15. You get no points for a higher AUROC, for choosing a particular model or for visual polish: a plain app with a well-reasoned design choice earns full credit. After the deadline, every app goes into a class gallery. Full rubric

Fair play

The protocol

  • Use the supplied split. Never reshuffle, and never fit a model on test rows. {{help five-rules}}
  • Keep preprocessing inside the pipeline, so it learns from the training rows only.
  • Never edit results by hand. Your app computes every number from results/ through m11.

AI use

  • Your agent writes and debugs the code. AGENT_BRIEF.md keeps it to the protocol and tells it to leave your decisions and answers to you.
  • You make every decision in DECISIONS.md yourself, and you write your Canvas answers in your own words.
  • Check what the agent gives you: run the checks and the app yourself. When you submit, say which AI tools you used.
  • Ask your agent for options, not answers. In DECISIONS.md you record one AI suggestion you turned down, and your design choice must be your own idea.
  • Follow the course AI policy for anything not covered here.
  • Optional and ungraded: save your prompts in prompts/, one file per session.
