Canvas questions (preview)

Answer these on Canvas: 100–150 words each, in your own words, citing numbers from your app. Say which partition each number comes from: development (OOF) or test.

  1. Does the readmission model help? (Section 1) Report the development accuracy of “always no” and of one trained model at 0.50. Give that model's TP, FP, and FN. Would accuracy alone lead a hospital to the right decision? Name the metric that changes your answer, and explain what it shows in patients per 100 discharges.
  2. What did your threshold change? (Section 2) For your diabetes model, compare 0.50 with your saved threshold. Report both confusion matrices, plus the sensitivity, precision, and number flagged per 100 adults. Who carries the extra work? Why did AUROC stay the same? Did the threshold reach 0.90 sensitivity on the test set?
  3. Is a good ranking a trustworthy probability? (Section 3) Pick two models on one dataset with similar AUROC. Compare their calibration plots. Would AUROC alone justify telling a patient “your risk is 80%”? Which model would you let quote a risk, and why? If the plots look alike, say so.
  4. Is the best model really better? (Section 4) On heart, compare the model with the highest CV AUROC to logistic regression. Is the lead within one SD? Which model does the lecture's rule favour? How does the small sample show up in the fold SD and the test result? Compare this with the prediction you saved before results.

No points for higher AUROC, for choosing a particular model, for visual polish, or for a correct prediction before results.

False claims to avoid

None of these may appear anywhere in your app:

  • AUROC described as “accuracy” {{help auroc}}
  • an SVM score described as a probability {{help raw-score}}
  • a CV result labelled as a test result {{help cv-auroc}}
  • a causal claim about a feature
