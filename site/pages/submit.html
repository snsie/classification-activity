Canvas questions (preview)

Answer these on Canvas: 100–150 words each, in your own words, citing numbers from your app. Say which partition each number comes from: training or test.

  1. Does the readmission model help? (Section 1) Report the test accuracy of “always no” and of one trained model at 0.50. Give that model's TP, FP, and FN. Would accuracy alone lead a hospital to the right decision? Name the metric that changes your answer, and explain what it shows in patients per 100 discharges.
  2. What does a threshold for the job cost? (Section 2) For one diabetes model, compare 0.50 with a threshold that reaches at least 0.90 sensitivity on the test set. Report both confusion matrices, plus the sensitivity, precision, and number flagged per 100 adults. Who carries the extra work? Why did AUROC stay the same? Why might new adults get slightly less than 0.90?
  3. Which model would you trust on new patients? (Sections 3 and 4) On one dataset, compare random forest with logistic regression: their training AUROC, test AUROC and the gap. Which one memorised its training patients, and how can you tell? Which would you recommend to the clinical lead, and what would you warn them about?

Rubric Suggested weights

App25All four required minimums met, readable cold by a clinical lead, labelled partitions, no false claims, recommendations shown
Design choice and AI use5One design choice of your own, visible in the app, with a reason tied to your clinical lead and the job; one AI suggestion you turned down, with a reason (or “no AI tools used”)
Canvas reasoning45Own numbers, correct interpretation, tied to the job, uncertainty stated (15 each)

No points for higher AUROC, for choosing a particular model or for visual polish. The design-choice points reward a deliberate, explained decision, not a good-looking app: “I put flagged adults per 100 first because the clinical lead staffs the clinic” earns full credit in a plain app.

Class gallery Ungraded

After the deadline, every app goes into a class gallery. Open a few of your classmates' apps and vote in three categories:

  • The app a clinical lead would actually open: the reader could make the decision from it, cold.
  • Most honest about uncertainty: it shows what the model can't do, not only what it can.
  • Clearest threshold trade-off: it makes plain what moving the threshold gains, and who carries the cost.

Votes don't change your grade. The winners are shown to the class, with their design choices.

False claims to avoid

None of these may appear anywhere in your app:

  • AUROC described as “accuracy” {{help auroc}}
  • a training result labelled as a test result {{help training-auroc}}
  • a causal claim about a feature
