Never in your app

  • AUROC described as “accuracy”.
  • A training result labelled as a test result.
  • A causal claim about a feature.

Ideas, not templates Optional

  • Draw 100 patients as dots, sorted into the four cells, so “of the patients who…” is something you can count.
  • Put “always no” and the model side by side: same accuracy, very different catches.
  • Say each metric as the clinical lead's question: “Of the patients we flag, how many come back?”
  • Let the comparison follow Section 2's slider, so the lead can compare the models at the job's threshold too.

Accurate, readable tables meet every required minimum. Custom visuals are optional.

Common questions

Which way round does the matrix go?

The course layout: rows are what actually happened, columns are what the model said, positives first. scikit-learn's confusion_matrix uses another order, so label both axes.

The baseline flags no one. Is precision 0?

No: with no one flagged, precision is 0 ÷ 0, which is undefined. Show “undefined”.

Can I say a feature raises the risk of readmission?

No. The models learned associations in historical data. Say “patients with more past admissions tended to get higher probabilities”, never “admissions cause readmission”.
